Welcome to Beacon Park, the green heart of Lichfield. While it’s a peaceful spot today, this land has a bit of a "identity crisis" in its history.

For over five hundred years, the road next to you wasn't called Beacon Street—it was Bacon Street. Local legend says a signal beacon once stood here to warn of the Spanish Armada, but most historians think that's just a tall tale. In reality, the name was likely changed in 1800 by a local homeowner who thought "Beacon" sounded much more dignified for his estate than a cut of pork!

As you walk toward the Museum Gardens, you’ll encounter a figure who seems a long way from home: Captain Edward Smith of the RMS Titanic. Though he was born in the Potteries, his statue was placed here in 1914. Why? Because Lichfield was a bustling stop for travelers moving between London and the Great Liverpool docks. It was designed to be a prominent memorial for international visitors to honor the man who went down with his ship.


But look toward the Cathedral spires. In the 1640s, this wasn't a park—it was a war zone. During the English Civil War, the Cathedral was a fortified Royalist stronghold. The ground you’re standing on was used by Oliver Cromwell’s troops and later Prince Rupert to launch a brutal siege. If you look closely at the Cathedral walls later today, you can still see the pockmarks from the cannons that were fired from this very direction.

From medieval sieges to Victorian strolls, Beacon Park is a testament to Lichfield’s resilience. Enjoy the gardens, and keep an eye out for the ghost of the old "Bacon Street"!
